pdfdol.figures#

Crop a figure or table out of a PDF by its caption, and locate a quoted passage.

Two things a caller illustrating a paper needs:

  • figure(pdf, "Figure 3:") returns the figure as PNG bytes, found by its caption. figure_box says where it is (FigureBox(page, box, source)) without rendering it.

  • find_passage(pdf, quote) returns the page and the boxes of a quoted passage, so it can be highlighted (Passage(page, boxes, source)).

Pages are 0-based (like the page lists PdfFilesReader gives; checks.text_overlaps reports 1-based page numbers for people). Boxes are (x0, y0, x1, y1) in PDF points, origin at the top left of the unrotated page, which is how PyMuPDF reports text and drawings; figure converts for a rotated page when it renders.

How a figure is found, in order: the graphics above its caption, else short text blocks above it (a figure drawn as text), else the graphics beside it (a side caption). Growth from the caption joins graphics no more than max_gap apart, and never across another caption or a body paragraph, so a figure does not swallow its neighbour. A table is the table PyMuPDF detects next to its caption, else its horizontal rules (booktabs), else the nearer graphics; source names which, and "caption-only" or "fallback" says nothing better was found.

Scanned PDFs (a page covered by one image, its text layer made by OCR) are a known trap: the text layer’s boxes can sit tens of pixels off the glyphs. find_passage detects such pages and, unless it is given an ocr_words function to read word boxes off the rendered pixels (ocracy_words is one, built on ocracy), returns the page with no boxes rather than a highlight in the wrong place. Figures on scanned pages are best effort.

Needs PyMuPDF, the figures extra of pdfdol, imported only when called. PyMuPDF is AGPL-3.0: whoever distributes or serves pdfdol together with it takes on its terms.

The caption-matching rule (a caption is text that starts with the given caption, not a mention of it, and not a longer label) needs no PDF to check:

>>> _starts_caption("Figure 1: Growth of the thing", "Figure 1:")
True
>>> _starts_caption("Figure 10: Another one", "Figure 1")
False
>>> _starts_caption("TABLE II Results", "Table I")
False
>>> _starts_caption("As Figure 1: shows", "Figure 1:")
False

and neither does the matching find_passage uses (case, curly quotes, punctuation, dashes and hyphens do not matter):

>>> _match_tokens([("The", 1), ("AI’s", 2), ("repos-", 3), ("itories,", 4)], "ai's repositories")
[2, 3, 4]

pdfdol.figures.figure_box(pdf, caption, *, page=None, kind=None, with_caption=True, margin=6.0, max_up=0.65, max_gap=40.0, table_height=0.35, rule_gap=0.3, body_words=25)[source]#

Where the figure or table captioned caption is: FigureBox(page, box, source).

Parameters:

  • pdf – path, bytes, file object, or an open PyMuPDF document.

  • caption (str) – the start of the caption, e.g. "Figure 3:" or "Table 2". Matched case-insensitively at the start of a text block or line, and only where the label reads as a caption (“Table 2 shows” and “Table 2)” are prose). Of several matches, the first whose figure or table is found is used.

  • page (int | None) – 0-based page to look on; every page when None.

  • kind (str | None) – "figure" or "table"; when None, "table" if the caption starts with “Tab”, else "figure".

  • with_caption (bool) – include the caption in the box. Without it, a caption with nothing found next to it raises LookupError (there is no figure to return).

  • margin (float) – white space (points) kept around the box.

  • max_up (float) – how far from the caption, as a fraction of the page height, a figure’s graphics may reach.

  • max_gap (float) – gap (points) that separates a figure from other graphics.

  • table_height (float) – fraction of the page taken below a table caption when nothing is found next to it (source="fallback").

  • rule_gap (float) – fraction of the page height two consecutive rules of one table may be apart.

  • body_words (int) – a text block with at least this many words is body text: a figure never grows across it.

Raises:

  • LookupError – no caption starts with caption (or, with_caption=False, nothing was found next to it).

  • IndexError – page is not a page of the PDF.

Return type:

FigureBox

pdfdol.figures.find_passage(pdf, quote, *, page=None, ocr_words=None, ocr_zoom=3.0, scan_coverage=0.8, head_words=6)[source]#

The page and boxes of quote in pdf, or None when it is not there.

Matches the quote against the page’s words by their letters and digits, so case, curly quotes, punctuation, dashes and hyphens (compounds, and words broken at a line end) do not matter. Every page is searched for the whole quote before any is searched for its first head_words words (source="text-head"). On a scanned page (images cover scan_coverage of it) the text layer’s boxes are not trusted: they are read off the rendered page with ocr_words when given (accepted only if the OCR’d words spell the quote), else the page is returned with no boxes (source="none"). With ocr_words, a scanned page with no text layer at all is OCR’d too (only those: OCR is slow).

Parameters:

  • pdf – path, bytes, file object, or an open PyMuPDF document.

  • quote (str) – the text to find.

  • page (int | None) – 0-based page to look on; every page when None.

  • ocr_words (Callable[[bytes], Iterable[tuple]] | None) – png_bytes -> [(word, (x0, y0, x1, y1)), ...] in pixels of that image, for scanned pages. ocracy_words is one.

  • ocr_zoom (float) – rendering scale of the page given to ocr_words.

Return type:

Passage | None

>>> p = find_passage("paper.pdf", "the results hold across models")
>>> p.page, p.boxes[0]
(4, (72.0, 310.2, 523.1, 322.0))
